# Question 24: Fair Productivity Comparison Between Teams

**Topics:** Metric Normalization, Productivity, Fair Comparison

## 📝 Question

> Two sales teams with different numbers of employees report their performance. For a fair comparison of their productivity, assuming similar working hours and conditions, which metric is more suitable?

## 🔢 Options

**Option 1:** Revenue or number of sales per salesperson

**Option 2:** The number of meetings logged by each team's manager

**Option 3:** The total number of the organization's customers

**Option 4:** Each team's total revenue regardless of the number of employees

## 💡 About this question

A fair comparison means removing the effect of team size. A larger team naturally has higher total revenue without necessarily being more efficient. For productivity we must **normalize** the metric, for example **revenue (or number of sales) per person**.

## 🔍 Detailed review of every option

### Option 1 — ✅ Correct

> Revenue or number of sales per salesperson

**Correct.** Revenue or number of sales per salesperson cancels out the effect of headcount and compares productivity fairly.

### Option 2 — ❌ Incorrect

> The number of meetings logged by each team's manager

**Incorrect.** The number of meetings logged by the manager is an activity indicator, not a sales output/productivity measure; it has no direct link to sales results.

### Option 3 — ❌ Incorrect

> The total number of the organization's customers

**Incorrect.** The organization's total number of customers is the same for both teams (or unrelated to team performance) and does not show differences in productivity.

### Option 4 — ❌ Incorrect

> Each team's total revenue regardless of the number of employees

**Incorrect.** Total revenue regardless of headcount is biased in favor of the larger team and does not show productivity.

## ✅ Correct answer

**Option 1**

> Revenue or number of sales per salesperson

## 🎯 Why it is correct

Productivity = output ÷ input (e.g., revenue ÷ number of salespeople). Only option 1 removes the effect of team size.

## 📌 Key takeaway

To compare unequal groups, normalize the metric (per person, per hour, etc.).
