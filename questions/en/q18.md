# Question 18: Successive Percentage Increase and Decrease

**Topics:** Percentages, Relative Change, Base of Calculation

## 📝 Question

> If sales increase by 20% in the first month and decrease by 20% in the second month, what is the change in sales relative to the start of the first month?

## 🔢 Options

**Option 1:** 4% decrease

**Option 2:** 4% increase

**Option 3:** No change

**Option 4:** 2% decrease

## 💡 About this question

Percentage increases and decreases do not share a base: the second 20% is applied to the **new** (larger) value.

Assume initial sales of 100:

- Month one: `100 × 1.2 = 120`
- Month two: `120 × 0.8 = 96`

Result: `96` versus `100`, i.e. a **4% decrease**. (In general `1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96`)

## 🔍 Detailed review of every option

### Option 1 — ✅ Correct

> 4% decrease

**Correct.** `1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96`; final sales are 96% of the initial value, i.e. a 4% decrease.

### Option 2 — ❌ Incorrect

> 4% increase

**Incorrect.** It is not an increase; a 20% decrease on the larger value (120) is bigger than a 20% increase on the smaller value (100).

### Option 3 — ❌ Incorrect

> No change

**Incorrect.** "No change" would hold if the decrease and increase shared a base. Here the bases differ (100 vs 120).

### Option 4 — ❌ Incorrect

> 2% decrease

**Incorrect.** A 2% decrease is not the result of the correct calculation; the actual size of the drop is `100 − 96 = 4`.

## ✅ Correct answer

**Option 1**

> 4% decrease

## 🎯 Why it is correct

Percentage changes multiply, they do not add: `1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96` ⇒ 4% decrease.

## 📌 Key takeaway

Multiply successive percentage changes; +20% and −20% do not cancel out.
