# Question 12: WHERE vs HAVING

**Topics:** SQL, WHERE, HAVING, GROUP BY

## 📝 Question

> What is the difference between WHERE and HAVING in SQL?

## 🔢 Options

**Option 1:** All items are correct

**Option 2:** WHERE works only on text columns

**Option 3:** HAVING works only with JOIN

**Option 4:** WHERE filters rows before grouping and HAVING filters groups after aggregation

## 💡 About this question

Logical execution order of a query: `FROM/JOIN → WHERE → GROUP BY → HAVING → SELECT → ORDER BY`.

- **WHERE** filters rows **before grouping** and cannot place conditions on aggregate results.
- **HAVING** filters **groups after aggregation** and can place conditions on `SUM`, `COUNT`, etc.

## 🔍 Detailed review of every option

### Option 1 — ❌ Incorrect

> All items are correct

**Incorrect.** "All items are correct" would hold only if all the other options were true, but options 2 and 3 are false.

### Option 2 — ❌ Incorrect

> WHERE works only on text columns

**Incorrect.** WHERE works on any column type (numeric, text, date, etc.), not only text.

### Option 3 — ❌ Incorrect

> HAVING works only with JOIN

**Incorrect.** HAVING does not depend on JOIN; it is used with `GROUP BY` (and even without it, treating the whole table as one group).

### Option 4 — ✅ Correct

> WHERE filters rows before grouping and HAVING filters groups after aggregation

**Correct.** WHERE filters rows before grouping and HAVING filters the aggregated result (the groups). Example: `WHERE year = 2024 ... GROUP BY region HAVING SUM(amount) > 1000`.

## ✅ Correct answer

**Option 4**

> WHERE filters rows before grouping and HAVING filters groups after aggregation

## 🎯 Why it is correct

WHERE acts on raw rows (before GROUP BY) and HAVING on aggregated groups (after GROUP BY).

## 📌 Key takeaway

Condition on a raw column → WHERE; condition on an aggregate → HAVING.
