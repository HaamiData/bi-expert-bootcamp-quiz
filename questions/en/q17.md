# Question 17: Data Cleaning: Standardizing City Names

**Topics:** Data Cleaning, Data Quality, Standardization

## 📝 Question

> If the name of a city has been recorded in different forms such as “تهران”, “تهران ” (with a trailing space) and “TEHRAN”, what should be done to count customers accurately by city?

## 🔢 Options

**Option 1:** Standardizing the values by cleaning up spaces and defining standard naming rules

**Option 2:** Converting the city name to random numbers

**Option 3:** Counting each form as a separate city

**Option 4:** Deleting all rows related to this city

## 💡 About this question

Spelling variants, extra spaces and language/case differences cause one entity to be counted several times and make per-city counts wrong. The principled fix is **data cleaning and standardization**: trim extra spaces, normalize case and language, and define naming rules or a mapping table of valid values.

## 🔍 Detailed review of every option

### Option 1 — ✅ Correct

> Standardizing the values by cleaning up spaces and defining standard naming rules

**Correct.** Trimming spaces and unifying the different forms (تهران, TEHRAN) with standard rules makes each city a single value and the count accurate. These rules are best applied in ETL/Power Query and the source data should be fixed too.

### Option 2 — ❌ Incorrect

> Converting the city name to random numbers

**Incorrect.** Converting city names to random numbers destroys information and does not solve the problem; even different forms of the same city would get different numbers.

### Option 3 — ❌ Incorrect

> Counting each form as a separate city

**Incorrect.** Counting each form as an independent city is exactly the error: one city is counted several times and customers are split among the forms.

### Option 4 — ❌ Incorrect

> Deleting all rows related to this city

**Incorrect.** Deleting rows loses real customers and biases the analysis. The problem lies in the recording format, not in the existence of the data.

## ✅ Correct answer

**Option 1**

> Standardizing the values by cleaning up spaces and defining standard naming rules

## 🎯 Why it is correct

The problem is inconsistent representation of one value; the fix is to standardize the values.

## 📌 Key takeaway

Before aggregating, trim and normalize categorical data and define standard rules.
