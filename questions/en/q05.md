# Question 5: Median for Salaries with an Outlier

**Topics:** Descriptive Statistics, Measures of Central Tendency, Outliers

## 📝 Question

> For reporting the salaries of employees of a company whose CEO's salary is far higher than everyone else's, which measure of central tendency is more appropriate?

## 🔢 Options

**Option 1:** Median

**Option 2:** Mean

**Option 3:** Bias

**Option 4:** Variance

## 💡 About this question

When data contains an **outlier**, the mean is pulled toward it and gives a misleading picture of the "typical salary." The **median** is the middle value of the sorted data and is robust against outliers.

The other two options are not measures of central tendency at all: "bias" is a systematic-error concept and "variance" is a measure of dispersion.

## 🔍 Detailed review of every option

### Option 1 — ✅ Correct

> Median

**Correct.** The median is not affected by the CEO's very high salary and better shows the "typical" salary of employees.

### Option 2 — ❌ Incorrect

> Mean

**Incorrect.** The mean is strongly inflated by one very large value (the CEO's salary) and yields a number that represents none of the ordinary employees.

### Option 3 — ❌ Incorrect

> Bias

**Incorrect.** Bias is not a measure of central tendency; it refers to a systematic error in an estimate or model.

### Option 4 — ❌ Incorrect

> Variance

**Incorrect.** Variance is a measure of **dispersion**, not central tendency; it shows how far data lies from the mean, not what the "typical salary" is.

## ✅ Correct answer

**Option 1**

> Median

## 🎯 Why it is correct

For data with outliers, the median is the robust and appropriate measure of central tendency.

## 📌 Key takeaway

Outliers or skewed distribution → median; symmetric data without outliers → mean.
